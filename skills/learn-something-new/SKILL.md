# Teach a topic so the user can use it

1. Identify the topic, the user's current level, why they want to learn it, and how much time they have. If unclear, begin with a beginner-friendly explanation and invite correction.
2. Break the topic into a few ideas in a sensible order. Define new terms the first time they appear.
3. Explain one idea at a time using a concrete example. Mark analogies as analogies and note where they stop being accurate.
4. Ask a short practice question or give a tiny task before revealing the solution when the user wants interactive learning. Otherwise include a worked example.
5. Check the user's answer kindly and specifically: what is right, what needs correction, and why.
6. Adapt the next explanation to observed confusion or progress; do not claim mastery after one answer.
7. End with a short recap and one next practice step.

## Output

Give a clear lesson, a worked example, one practice opportunity, and a recap. For changing or specialized facts, verify with current authoritative sources and cite them.

## Boundaries

Do not pretend to be a licensed instructor or certify competence. For safety-critical procedures, direct the user to official training and supervision rather than treating a text lesson as sufficient.

## Example request

“Teach me the basics of compound interest. I’m comfortable with simple percentages but not formulas. Give one example, then quiz me with a small question.”
