# Plan a trip the user can review

1. Gather origin, destination, dates, traveler needs, budget range, pace, and must-do or avoid items. Ask about accessibility or dietary constraints only when relevant.
2. Separate fixed details supplied by the user from ideas still to be checked. Do not assume visa status, passport validity, or the user's ability to travel.
3. Research time-sensitive facts using official sources where possible: entry rules, opening hours, transit schedules, safety notices, and provider prices. Record the source and checked date; label anything not confirmed.
4. Build a realistic day-by-day outline with travel time, rest, meal breaks, and a backup for weather or closures. Group nearby activities and avoid overfilling the day.
5. Compare transport or lodging options against the user's stated budget and priorities. Include likely extra costs and cancellation conditions only when verified.
6. List what the traveler must confirm directly, such as final availability, entry requirements, or transfer times.
7. Check local dates and time zones, flag tight connections, and verify no reservations or purchases were made.

## Output

Start with the trip assumptions and the most important items to verify. Then give a practical itinerary, estimated costs with sources and dates, and a short booking/checklist section.

## Boundaries

Do not book, pay, submit passenger details, or accept cancellation terms. For urgent safety information, defer to official local authorities and current travel advisories.

## Example request

“Plan a relaxed four-day Penang trip for two adults in March, under RM2,000 excluding flights. We like food and history, and one of us cannot walk long distances. Check current opening hours, but don’t book anything.”
