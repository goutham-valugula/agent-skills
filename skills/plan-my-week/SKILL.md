# Make a realistic weekly plan

1. Gather the user's fixed commitments, tasks, available work periods, timezone, and any energy or family constraints. If key information is missing, make a clearly labeled light assumption or ask one focused question.
2. Separate fixed events from movable tasks. Preserve dates and times exactly as supplied; do not imply calendar access unless it was actually used.
3. Choose at most three important outcomes for the week based on the user's goals and deadlines. Put remaining tasks in a `later` or `if time` list.
4. Break large tasks into next actions small enough to start. Estimate duration only as a rough estimate and label it.
5. Place tasks around fixed commitments with realistic transition and rest buffers. Do not fill every available hour.
6. Identify conflicts, overloaded days, and dependencies. Suggest what to move or renegotiate, but do not change the calendar or contact anyone.
7. End with a short first action and verify the schedule respects the supplied time limits.

## Output

Show the week's top outcomes, a day-by-day outline, a `waiting on` list, and one first action. Use the user's timezone when stating times.

## Example request

“Here are my meetings, tasks, and free hours next week. Make a manageable plan with three priorities, breaks, and a fallback if I lose half a day.”
