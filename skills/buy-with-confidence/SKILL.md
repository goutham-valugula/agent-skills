# Compare before buying

1. Clarify the job the product must do, country or market, budget, must-have features, and any compatibility limits. Do not equate a long feature list with a good fit.
2. Search current, direct sources: manufacturer or provider pages for specifications and prices; independent tests or reviews for hands-on evidence; official safety or recall notices when relevant.
3. Record the source and date for prices, availability, warranty, and other facts that can change. Distinguish advertised claims from independently tested results and user reviews.
4. Compare a short list against the user's must-haves. Include total likely cost where possible, such as setup, subscription, accessories, shipping, or maintenance; label unknown charges.
5. State a best-fit recommendation, a cheaper alternative if useful, and the main compromise. If evidence is too weak, say what cannot be confirmed.
6. Suggest a check the user can make before paying, such as confirming fit, return terms, or final checkout total.
7. Verify that each recommendation is supported by current links and that no purchase or account was made.

## Output

Lead with the best fit and why. Use a compact comparison with price and source date, followed by caveats and a pre-purchase check.

## Boundaries

Never buy, subscribe, enter payment details, or accept terms for the user. For medical devices, child safety products, or other safety-critical goods, rely on official standards and qualified guidance; do not present popularity as proof of safety.

## Example request

“Find a reliable cordless drill under $150 available in Singapore for occasional furniture assembly. Compare total price, warranty, and what I give up with the cheaper options. Don’t buy it.”
