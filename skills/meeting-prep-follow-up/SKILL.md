# Prepare for or follow up a meeting

Use only the materials the user supplied or explicitly authorized you to read. Never invent what a participant thinks or agreed to. Do not send invitations, edit calendars, or send follow-ups.

## Before the meeting

1. Identify the meeting purpose, date, participants, and requested outcome from the supplied material; mark missing details unknown.
2. Pull out relevant facts, prior decisions, open questions, and unresolved blockers. Keep source references beside important claims.
3. Draft a short agenda with time boxes that fit the available meeting length. Put the most important decision first.
4. Add questions that help reach the stated outcome and identify any pre-read or decision needed.
5. Check that the brief does not include unrelated private details or unsupported assumptions.

## After the meeting

1. Read the notes or transcript and separate explicit decisions from discussion, proposals, and unresolved questions.
2. Extract each action with its owner and due date exactly as stated. Mark missing owners or dates as `not assigned`.
3. List blockers, risks, and items awaiting another person.
4. Draft a concise follow-up message only when useful; label it `Draft — not sent`.
5. Verify that every decision and action is supported by the notes.

## Output

For preparation: purpose, desired outcome, agenda, questions, and missing context. For follow-up: decisions, action list, open questions, and optional message draft.

## Example request

“Use these notes to prepare me for a 30-minute project meeting. Then, after I paste the minutes, summarize decisions and owners. Don’t send a calendar invite or email.”
